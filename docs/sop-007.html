California Forest Pest Council: 2026 Governance Framework & Bylaws Revision

SOP-007: Field Safety

**ARTICLE I. PURPOSE AND GOVERNING AUTHORITY

  1. Purpose: This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) operationalizes Section 4.06(b) (Conference Committee) and Section 7.02 (Full Council Annual Meeting) of the California Forest Pest Council (CFPC) Bylaws. It provides a structured framework for program development, registration management, venue contracting, continuing education accreditation, and field tour safety risk mitigation.
  2. Governing Scope: Applies to the Conference Committee, Technical Committee Chairs, Council Officers, event logistics staff, and all attendees participating in Council-sponsored meetings and outdoor field tours.

**ARTICLE II. CONFERENCE COMMITTEE STRUCTURE AND TIMELINE

  1. Committee Composition (§ 4.06b): The Conference Committee shall consist of a Chairperson appointed by the Executive Committee, supported by designated coordinators for Program Agenda, Local Arrangements, Registration, Financial Accounts, and Continuing Education.
  2. Annual Planning Timeline:
Planning Phase Target Schedule Key Deliverables & Action Items
Phase 1: Location & Dates January – February Select host region, secure meeting facility/lodging contracts, and draft preliminary field tour routes.
Phase 2: Program & CEUs March – June Finalize session topics with Technical Committee Chairs; apply for RPF (Registered Professional Forester) and PCA (Pest Control Advisor) Continuing Education Units.
Phase 3: Registration Launch July 15 Publish formal Meeting Notice (Bylaws § 7.01), launch online registration, and distribute call for presentations.
Phase 4: Site Scouting & Safety August – September Perform physical walkthrough of field tour stops; secure private land access permits and emergency medical contacts.
Phase 5: Event Execution October / November Execute the Fall Annual Meeting, Technical Sessions, Field Tour, and General Business Meeting (Bylaws § 7.02).
Phase 6: Post-Event Wrap-Up Within 30 Days Reconcile event revenues/expenses with Treasurer (SOP-002) and submit attendee rosters to Secretary (Bylaws § 6.03).

**ARTICLE III. REGISTRATION MANAGEMENT & VOTING ELIGIBILITY

  1. Registration Fee Structure: The Conference Committee, in consultation with the Treasurer, shall establish registration fees to cover venue costs, catering, field transportation, and proceedings printing.
  2. Eligibility Roster Synchronization:
    • Same-day physical and electronic registration desks must log attendee contact details, organizational affiliation, and professional capacity.
    • Pursuant to Bylaws Section 6.01(a), attending the Annual Meeting or Technical Field Tour immediately establishes or renews the attendee’s 2-year CFPC voting eligibility.
  3. Ethics Affirmation Integration: Registration materials (online web forms and physical sign-in sheets) shall include the mandatory affirmation checkbox: “By registering, I agree to support the mission of the CFPC and abide by the Council Code of Ethics (SOP-001).”

**ARTICLE IV. CONTINUING EDUCATION UNITS (CEU) ACCREDITATION

To maintain professional value for natural resource practitioners, the Conference Committee shall submit educational agendas to accredited licensing bodies:

  1. California Department of Pesticide Regulation (DPR): Submit pesticide laws/regulations and pest management content for Pest Control Advisor (PCA), Qualified Applicator (QAC/QAL), and Private Applicator credits.
  2. California Board of Forestry and Fire Protection: Submit forestry management, silviculture, and pest impact sessions for Registered Professional Forester (RPF) continuing education credits.
  3. Verification & Certificates: Maintain daily sign-in/sign-out logs at the meeting venue and field stops, issuing completion certificates to verified participants within fifteen (15) days post-event.

**ARTICLE V. FIELD TOUR LOGISTICS AND SAFETY RISK MITIGATION

Outdoor field tours frequently traverse active timber operations, rugged terrain, steep slopes, and remote wildland settings. The following safety protocols are mandatory for all field sessions:

Section 5.01 Scouting, Permits, and Land Access 1. Private/Agency Access Agreements:** Written permission or land entry permits must be secured from private timberland owners, Tribal natural resource departments, or public land agencies prior to finalizing tour stops. 2. Transportation Safety: If passenger vans or buses are hired, vendors must provide proof of commercial liability insurance. Caravan driving routes must include designated staging areas to prevent roadside traffic hazards.

Section 5.02 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) & Site Briefings 1. Mandatory PPE:** All field tour participants entering active forest management zones, logging sites, or hazardous terrain shall wear: * Hard hats (ANSI certified) * High-visibility safety vests (ANSI Class 2 or 3) * Sturdy, ankle-supporting work boots with non-slip soles * Eye and ear protection (when near operating equipment or active chipping/sampling) 2. Tailgate Safety Briefing: Before departing the initial staging area, the Field Tour Leader shall conduct a 5-minute safety briefing covering terrain hazards, active weather risks (heat/hypothermia), venomous species/poison oak, and vehicle convoy safety.

Section 5.03 Emergency Medical Response Plan 1. First Aid Requirements:** Every field tour vehicle/group leader must carry a fully stocked Class B Industrial First Aid Kit, trauma dressing, and communication equipment (satellite messenger/Garmin inReach or cell booster when operating outside cellular coverage). 2. Medical Evacuation Routing: The Field Coordinator shall map the nearest emergency medical facility for each tour stop and identify primary emergency access points for local emergency services (CAL FIRE / County EMS). 3. Incident Reporting: Any injury or property damage occurring during a Council-sponsored event must be reported in writing to the Council Chairperson and Treasurer within twenty-four (24) hours for insurance documentation (Bylaws § 3.07).
