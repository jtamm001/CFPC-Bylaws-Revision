Welcome to the interactive governance repository for the California Forest Pest Council (CFPC). This platform is structured to facilitate a transparent review of our organizational transition and is divided into three primary sections:

  1. Historical Archives: Preserving the legacy 1996 governance documents and foundational corporate records.
  2. Proposed 2026 Bylaws Revision: An interactive, side-by-side comparison of the structural updates to the Council’s governing framework.
  3. Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) Suite: A framework of administrative guidelines, committee protocols, and operational workflows.

A Note on the Standard Operating Procedures: The SOPs housed in this repository are currently working drafts. Because these documents govern the specific, day-to-day operations of the Council’s various technical and administrative bodies, they will not be enacted simultaneously with the core Bylaws. Instead, they will be developed and adopted progressively at a later date. This phased approach provides knowledgeable members and concerned stakeholders the critical time required to accurately document their institutional knowledge and submit refined operational procedures for Executive Committee consideration.

2 BYLAWS OF THE CALIFORNIA FOREST PEST Council

(Currently Approved)

A CALIFORNIA PUBLIC BENEFIT CORPORATION

ARTICLE 1 OBJECTIVES

SECTION 1. The California Forest Pest Council (herein referred to as the “Council”) is an organization, founded in 1951, of private and public forest managers, foresters, entomologists, pathologists, zoologists, biologists and others interested in the protection of forests from damage caused by animals, insects, diseases, and weeds. The objectives of the Council are to:

  1. Coordinate the detection, reporting, and compilation of pest damage.
  2. Evaluate pest conditions.
  3. Make recommendations on pest control to forest management and protection agencies and forest landowners.
  4. Review policy, legal and research aspects of forest pest management and submit recommendations thereon to appropriate authorities.
  5. Foster educational work on forest pests and forest health.

SECTION 2. The Council is recognized by the State Board of Forestry as the advisory body to it with respect to forest health protection matters, and is a participating member of the Western Forest Pest Committee of the Western Forestry and Conservation Association.

ARTICLE 2 MEMBERSHIP

SECTION 1. Participation in the Council is open to anyone having an interest in forest health protection. There is only one class of membership and all members have the same voting rights and privileges.

ARTICLE 3 OFFICERS

SECTION 1. The Council shall elect from its membership for a two year term of office a chairperson, vice-chairperson, and secretary. A treasurer, conference chairman and editorial chairman will be appointed annually by the Council chairperson to serve a one year term.

SECTION 2. Duties of the Chairperson. The chairperson shall preside at all meetings of the Council and the executive committee and shall perform any other duties or functions authorized by the executive committee. The chairperson may appoint ad hoc committees and their chairpersons, as deemed necessary to assist in carrying out the purposes of the Council. The chairperson is a non-voting member of all committees of the Council.

The Council Chairperson or other designated member of the executive committee or Council shall represent the Council, and report forest pest conditions and recommendations of the Council to the State Board of Forestry and appear on behalf of the Council before any other public bodies.

The Council Chairperson or other designated member of the executive committee or Council shall represent the Council, and report to, the annual meeting of the Western Forest Pest Committee of the Western Forestry and Conservation Association.

SECTION 3. Duties of Vice-Chairperson. In the absence of the chairperson, or in the event of his or her inability or refusal to act, the vice-chairperson shall perform all the duties of the chairperson.

SECTION 4. Duties of Secretary. The secretary shall be custodian of the records and keep the original, and a copy of these Bylaws, a book of minutes of all meetings of the executive committee, standing committees and Council business meetings. The secretary should also keep a copy of all conditions reports, proceedings and other publications distributed by the Council.

The secretary shall take minutes at the executive committee meetings and Council business meetings and distribute them to the executive committee within thirty days after the meeting.

The secretary shall see that all meeting notices are distributed in a timely fashion and maintain the membership list.

SECTION 5. Duties of Treasurer. The treasurer shall receive all payments, be custodian of funds of the Council, keep an account of all monies received and expended, and make commitments and disbursements authorized by the executive committee. At the annual business meeting and when asked by the executive committee, the treasurer shall make a full report covering the financial affairs of the Council. All funds, books, records and vouchers in the treasurer’s control should be subject to inspection and verification by the executive committee. The treasurer shall deliver to their successor all books, records, money and accounts pertaining to or belonging to the Council.

SECTION 6. Compensation. Council officers shall serve without compensation.

SECTION 7. Non-liability of Officers. The officers shall not be personally liable for the debts, liabilities, or other obligations of the Council.

ARTICLE 4 COMMITTEES

SECTION 1. Standing Committees. Standing committees are established as follows: - Animal Damage Committee - Disease Committee - Insect Committee - Weed Committee - Southern California Committee - Editorial Committee - Conference Committee - Executive Committee

SECTION 2. Membership on the Animal Damage, Disease, Insect, Weed and Southern California Committees shall be open to anyone interested in the subject matter of the Committee. The Animal Damage, Disease, Insect, Weed and Southern California Committees shall elect annually, from their membership, a chairperson, and a secretary.

SECTION 3. The Editorial Committee. The editorial committee shall be responsible for arranging for preparation and publication of an annual forest pest conditions report, proceedings of the annual CFPC meeting, and any other printed material of interest. The editorial committee will be chaired by one member representing the Director, California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection. The chairperson of the editorial committee will appoint the editor-in-Chief and any other members as needed.

SECTION 4. The Conference Committee. The conference committee with the assistance of the Council shall plan and carry out an effective annual meeting of the California Forest Pest Council. It shall consist of a chairperson and members to handle the program, local arrangements, registration, publicity and other matters. The conference committee chairperson is appointed by the Council chairperson. The conference chairperson may then appoint all committee members.

SECTION 5. The Executive Committee. The executive committee shall be composed of 15 members, as follows: 1. Executive committee chairperson - Council chairperson 2. Executive committee vice-chairperson - Council vice-chairperson 3. Executive committee secretary - Council secretary 4. Executive committee treasurer - Council treasurer 5. Member - animal damage committee chairperson 6. Member - disease committee chairperson 7. Member - insect committee chairperson 8. Member - weed committee chairperson 9. Member - Southern California committee chairperson 10. Members - editorial committee chairperson & editor-in-chief 11. Member - conference committee chairperson 12. Three members - at large, elected annually by the Council.

SECTION 6. In the event the above membership of the executive committee does not include one member each representing the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection, the Pacific Southwest Region of the USDA Forest Service, the University of California, and private industry, the Chairperson is authorized to appoint an additional person or persons as necessary to represent these organizations.

SECTION 7. Each executive committee member may designate an alternate to represent their interests in the event they can not attend an executive committee meeting.

SECTION 8. The executive committee shall coordinate all activities of the Council and its committees, and review recommendations for Council action made by the committees. It shall be empowered to take emergency action on behalf of the Council when necessary. No action may be taken on a matter which concerns the subject area of a standing committee unless the standing committee’s representative is present.

SECTION 9. The following 11 members of the executive committee have voting rights for matters that the executive committee votes upon: 1. Executive committee chairperson - Council chairperson 2. Executive committee vice-chairperson - Council vice-chairperson 3. Executive committee secretary - Council secretary 4. Member - animal damage committee chairperson 5. Member - disease committee chairperson 6. Member - insect committee chairperson 7. Member - weed committee chairperson 8. Member - Southern California committee chairperson 9. Three members - at large

Six voting members shall constitute a quorum if a representative from at least three of the following standing committees are present: animal damage, disease, insect, weed and Southern California.

SECTION 10. Executive committee members shall serve without compensation.

SECTION 11. The executive committee will serve as the advisory committee to the Board of Forestry.

SECTION 12. Ad hoc committees may be organized and serve at the pleasure of the Council chairperson or the executive committee.

ARTICLE 5 VOTING RIGHTS

SECTION 1. Voting rights at the annual Council meeting and standing committee meetings will be established by attendance at the previous year’s Council or standing committee meeting, by attendance at a field meeting of a standing committee the current year, or by application to the Council secretary sixty days prior to the annual Council meeting. Any rights so established will remain valid for two years without further participation.

Each member is entitled to one vote on each matter submitted to a vote by the members. Voting at meetings shall be by voice vote with majority rules. Election of Officers, however, shall be by ballot if the results of the voice vote are not clear. Each member of a standing committee shall vote for officers of only one committee.

SECTION 2. Rosters. The Council secretary shall maintain and annually distribute to members of the executive committee: 1. Names and addresses of standing committee officers and of the full membership of the conference, editorial and executive committee. 2. Names and addresses of voting members thirty days prior the annual meeting of the Council.

The animal damage, disease, insect, weed and Southern California committees shall prepare and maintain lists of members interested in their subject matter field, and send copies to the Council secretary.

ARTICLE 6 NOTICE OF MEETINGS

SECTION 1. The Council secretary shall prepare and distribute notices of Council meetings to the membership.

SECTION 2. Standing committee chairpersons or secretaries shall handle distribution of notices of their meetings to those interested, sending copies also to members of the executive committee.

ARTICLE 7 MEETINGS

SECTION 1. Full Council Meetings. The full Council shall meet at least annually in November. The animal damage, disease, insect, weed and Southern California committees shall meet at least annually, and prior to the annual Council meeting, to review pest conditions in their subject area and to develop recommendations thereon to the Council.

SECTION 2. Executive committee meetings. Regular meetings of the executive committee shall be held in February, August and November. In November, the executive committee shall meet just prior to the annual Council meeting, to review and coordinate recommendations being submitted by committees to the Council.

SECTION 3. Conduct of Meetings. Meetings of the Council and committees shall be presided over by the appropriate chairperson. Meetings shall be governed by “Roberts’ Rules of Order”, revised version.

SECTION 4. Minutes. The Council secretary shall prepare and distribute minutes of Council meetings within thirty days thereof to members of the executive committee. Copies of the minutes may be distributed to other members with notices or at any subsequent meetings. The Council secretary shall send copies of resolutions or other important material being submitted to external bodies, as a result of Council action, to members of the executive committee.

The chairpersons or secretaries of the animal damage, disease, insect, weed and Southern California committees shall distribute copies of any minutes prepared of their meetings, and of any other pertinent correspondences and material, to the Secretary of the executive committee.

ARTICLE 8 RESOLUTIONS

SECTION 1. Resolutions. Resolutions are the means by which the Council advocates action or states positions on forest pest and forest health matters.

Resolutions shall be introduced for discussion and adoption in the appropriate subject area committee. The Committee chairperson will forward the resolution to the executive committee for review. The Committee chairperson or his or her designee will then present the resolution at the annual business meeting so the membership may discuss and vote on the resolution.

Resolutions shall include the names and addresses of intended recipients.

Emergency resolutions may be presented to the executive committee for a vote if the resolution deals with an urgent matter that can not wait until the November meeting of the full Council.

ARTICLE 9 FINANCES

SECTION 1. Expenditures. The executive committee may authorize expenditures of Council funds. Checks, orders for the payment of money, and other evidence of indebtedness of the Council shall be signed by the treasurer, or Council chairman, or other person designated by the Council chairman. The Council chairman may authorize the reimbursement of reasonable incidental expenses in support of Council business.

SECTION 2. Deposits. All funds of the Council shall be deposited from time to time to the credit of the Council in such banks, trust companies, or other depositories as the executive committee, or other person designated by the Council chairman may select.

SECTION 3. Contracts. The executive committee may authorize any officer or agent of the Council to enter into a contract on behalf of the Council. Unless so authorized, no officer, agent, or employee shall have any power or authority to bind the Council to any contract.

SECTION 4. Gifts. The Council chairman or the treasurer may accept on behalf of the Council any contribution, gift, or bequest for the charitable or public purposes of this Council.

SECTION 5. Fiscal year. The Council fiscal year shall begin on the 1st of January and end on the last day of December. (Note: Document original misnumbered this as Section 3; corrected for sequence).

ARTICLE 10 AMENDMENT OF BYLAWS OR ARTICLES OF INCORPORATION

SECTION 1. Amendments.

Changes to the Bylaws or Articles of Incorporation shall be presented to the executive committee for review and then presented to the full Council at the next regularly scheduled business meeting.

The By-laws and Articles of Incorporation may be amended by a majority vote of the eligible voting members present at the business meeting.

SOP-001: Code of Ethics Draft

The California Forest Pest Council (CFPC), founded in 1951, is a cooperative organization composed of individuals and organizations committed to the health and stewardship of California’s forests. Through education, scientific exchange, and collaboration, the Council promotes the understanding, prevention, detection, evaluation, and management of forest pests and other factors affecting forest health.

The Council serves as a forum for the exchange of scientific, technical, and practical knowledge and encourages informed decision-making related to forest health management. The Council recognizes that healthy forests provide important ecological, economic, cultural, and social benefits and that effective stewardship depends upon collaboration, sound science, and consideration of diverse perspectives.

To support this mission, members are expected to conduct themselves with honesty, objectivity, professionalism, and respect. This Code of Ethics establishes the principles that guide participation in Council activities and help ensure the Council’s work remains credible and effective.

Principles

  1. Stewardship of Forest Health

Members support the long-term health, resilience, and sustainability of California’s forests. Council activities shall be guided by sound science and responsible stewardship in understanding and managing forest pests and other threats to forest health.

  1. Scientific Integrity and Objectivity

Members shall support the use of sound science, accurate information, and professional judgment in Council activities. Members shall distinguish among facts, interpretations, opinions, and uncertainties and shall not knowingly misrepresent or distort information.

  1. Professional Competence and Education

Members participate in Council activities within the limits of their expertise and strive to maintain and enhance their professional knowledge and skills. Members support learning, mentorship, scientific inquiry, and the exchange of information among colleagues, students, and stakeholders.

  1. Objectivity

Members shall strive to be objective, fair, and evidence-based in their participation in Council activities. Members shall clearly distinguish personal or organizational views from positions officially adopted by the Council.

  1. Collaboration

Effective forest pest management and forest health stewardship benefit from diverse expertise, experience, and knowledge. Members shall foster open communication, constructive dialogue, mutual learning, and respectful consideration of differing viewpoints while engaging with others in a professional, courteous, and respectful manner.

  1. Integrity

Members shall conduct themselves honestly, ethically, and in good faith. Members are expected to uphold the integrity and purposes of the California Forest Pest Council and adhere to the Council’s bylaws and adopted policies.

  1. Professional Conduct in Meetings and Communications

Members shall participate in Council meetings and activities in a respectful, orderly, and constructive manner. Members shall follow the Council’s adopted meeting procedures and rules of order and contribute to fair and productive discussion. Disagreements shall focus on issues, information, and ideas rather than individuals. Communications related to Council business, whether verbal, written, electronic, or through social media, shall be conducted with professionalism, courtesy, and respect.

Application

This Code applies to all members, officers, committee participants, and individuals participating in activities of the California Forest Pest Council. Members are expected to uphold these principles while participating in Council meetings, committees, educational programs, publications, communications, and other Council-sponsored activities.

Membership in the Council reflects a shared interest in advancing the understanding and management of forest pests and forest health. Members support the purposes and mission of the California Forest Pest Council by contributing to the exchange of knowledge, experience, and ideas and by participating in Council activities in a professional, respectful, and constructive manner.

Violations of this Code may be reviewed and addressed in accordance with the Council’s bylaws, policies, and procedures.

SOP-003: Conditions Report Draft

ARTICLE I. PURPOSE AND GOVERNING AUTHORITY

  1. Purpose: This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) operationalizes Section 4.06(a) and Section 7.06 of the California Forest Pest Council (CFPC) Bylaws. It establishes formal guidelines for compiling, technical editing, publishing, and distributing the annual California Forest Pest Conditions Report and official meeting summaries.
  2. Governing Scope: Applies to the Editorial Committee, Technical Committee Chairs (Insects, Diseases, Invasive Vegetation, Animal Damage, Southern California), agency liaisons, and contributing forest health specialists.

ARTICLE II. EDITORIAL COMMITTEE LEADERSHIP & RESPONSIBILITIES

  1. Chairperson Appointment (§ 4.06a): The Editorial Committee Chair shall preferably be a professional representative designated by the Director of the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection (CAL FIRE).
  2. Core Responsibilities:
    • Establish annual data submission deadlines and standard reporting templates for technical contributors.
    • Conduct technical peer review and copyediting of all sub-committee condition summaries.
    • Manage document layout, spatial maps, photo permissions, and publication formatting.
    • Coordinate print and digital publication distribution to stakeholders, state regulators, and public archives.

ARTICLE III. REPORT STRUCTURE AND CONTENT STANDARDS

The annual California Forest Pest Conditions Report shall maintain a standardized, professional structure:

  1. Executive Summary & Key Trends: High-level narrative highlighting major mortality events, drought/climate interactions, and emerging invasive pest threats across California.
  2. Technical Committee Sections:
    • Forest Insects: Bark beetles, defoliators, wood borers, and invasive insect pests.
    • Forest Diseases: Native root diseases, foliar pathogens, cankers, and invasive pathogens (e.g., Phytophthora ramorum, Pitch Canker).

SOP-012: Membership Roster & Voting Eligibility

6.11 1. PURPOSE

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines the procedures for tracking California Forest Pest Council (CFPC) voting eligibility. It outlines how the Council Secretary maintains the membership list and how participants establish voting rights.

6.12 2. HOW TO EARN AND KEEP VOTING RIGHTS

Voting rights are non-transferable and valid for a rolling period of two (2) years. A participant can establish or renew their voting eligibility through one of two pathways:

  • Pathway A (Automatic via Attendance): Attend any CFPC-sponsored event (Annual Meeting, Technical Committee session, field tour, or workshop). Same-day registration or sign-in automatically grants voting rights for the subsequent 24 months.
  • Pathway B (Via Email Request): Professionals unable to attend an event may request voting rights by submitting an electronic eligibility request to the Council Secretary.

6.13 3. THE EMAIL REQUEST FORMAT

Professionals requesting voting rights under Pathway B must send a formal request to the Council Secretary containing the following information:

Subject: CFPC Voting Eligibility Request - [Full Name]

Please add me to the CFPC voting roster. * Full Name: * Primary Organization/Affiliation: (e.g., CAL FIRE, USFS, Tribal Nation, University) * Professional Role/Title: (e.g., Forester, Pathologist, Natural Resources Director) * Email Address: * Ethics Affirmation: “I support the CFPC’s mission and agree to abide by the Code of Ethics & Professional Conduct (SOP-001).”

6.14 4. THE MASTER ROSTER TRACKER

The Council Secretary shall maintain a centralized database (e.g., secure spreadsheet or SharePoint List) to track eligibility. The roster must track the following fields:

  • Name & Email Address
  • Organization & Title
  • Code of Ethics Agreement: (Verified / Not Verified)
  • Date of Last Qualifying Activity: (Date of last event attended or date of email request)
  • Eligibility Expiration Date: (Calculated as exactly 24 months from the Qualifying Activity Date)

6.15 5. MEETING CHECK-IN & SAME-DAY REGISTRATION

To ensure fluid participation during meetings without sacrificing regulatory integrity:

  1. Preparation: The Council Secretary shall provide the active roster to the registration desk prior to the event.
  2. Same-Day Walk-ins: Attendees registering at the door must complete the official CFPC Event Registration & Sign-In Sheet, which mandates the provision of core professional details and an explicit affirmation of SOP-001 (Code of Ethics).
  3. Immediate Voting Rights: Completion of the same-day registration sheet instantly grants the attendee voting privileges for that specific meeting.
  4. Post-Event Handover: Following the event, the registration desk must securely transmit all sign-in sheets to the Council Secretary, who will update the Master Roster Tracker for the upcoming two-year cycle.
